AS WRITTEN

5 of 11 units, or 10 with optimal local authoring. Either way it does not validate.

02What was verified

An evidence problem to start on now

Northeast Lakeview publishes no end-of-course outcomes for any course. Each catalog record carries a description, semester hours, a CIP code, and an "offered at" flag, and nothing more. Verified against the current 2026-2027 catalog.

No knowledge unit mapping can be produced from the catalog alone. Collecting current syllabi for every course in the plan is a blocking prerequisite for any CAE submission, not a clerical task for the end. It is free, and it gates everything else in this document.

The standard being applied, quoted from the NSA knowledge unit document:

"To complete each KU, an alignment to a minimum of 75% of the topics and sub-topics is required."

"While it is not required that every learning outcome be explicitly assessed as written, applicant schools should be able to defend their coverage of the learning outcomes."

BSP presents 8 top-level topics, the eighth carrying 10 sub-topics, for 18 elements. Seventy-five percent of 18 is 13.5, so the minimum is 14 of 18.

Two further rules shape the recommendation. A unit may be covered by more than one course, but a course "should not be aligned to an excessive number of KUs," and four or more courses on one unit "may be more of an indication of week [sic] KU coverage than strength because it risks diluting the KU." Two to three co-carriers per unit is the safe band. And: "A KU should not be aligned to elective or optional course(s)" , so every carrier named here must be required for all students.

3.3The displacement argument

Closing the gap to a defensible standard, meaning taught plus practiced plus evidenced in a graded artifact, is estimated at 26 instructional hours. Against a 64-hour course that is 41 percent of the whole course, and available slack is zero because the course already cut content to fit seven instructional weeks.

Taking the most favorable reading, in which 3 hours of already-compressed material counts as free, 21 hours must still come from the blocks carrying outcomes 2 through 6:

Combination reaching 21 hoursOutcomes destroyed or reduced
O3 (10.0) + O4 (9.0) = 19.0Two destroyed, and still short
O3 + O4 + O6 = 23.0Three destroyed
O3 + O4 + O2 extension = 25.0Two destroyed, one halved
O5 (8.0) + O4 + O2 extension = 23.0Three damaged
Anything avoiding O3, O4 and O5Caps at 14.0 hours. Fails, while already damaging three outcomes

Every combination that frees the required hours damages at least three of the six fixed outcomes. None reaching the requirement leaves all six intact. Two observations make it worse: reclaimed hours are credited one to one, which overstates the yield; and the largest block, the 11-hour Python foundation, is the block producing the 9 elements the course already covers, so spending it on BSP is net zero at best.

The course cannot simply be made larger either. At 2 lecture plus 2 lab it is 3.80 workload units. The contact hours required push it to 5.60 or 5.70, far outside the 3.9 ceiling, and enlarging it to 4 SCH raises a degree already at exactly 60 to 61.

3.4The assessment-integrity argument, which no redesign fixes

ITSY 1377 must permit AI assistance, for two reasons. The first is access: no programming prerequisite, and a blanket prohibition would fail most of a class that has never written code. The second is structural and stronger: outcome 6 requires students to evaluate AI-generated code for correctness before use. A course that forbids AI assistance cannot assess outcome 6, because outcome 6's object of study is AI-generated code. The permission is not a concession; it is load-bearing.

BSP requires the opposite posture. Four of its six outcomes begin "write and execute." An alignment review asks one question: which artifact demonstrates the student did this? In ITSY 1377 every code artifact is producible with AI assistance under an explicit policy. No artifact distinguishes a student who can write a loop from a student who can request a loop and run the result. Both are correctly assessed for this course's outcomes, which concern automating, extending, testing, documenting, and evaluating rather than authoring. The artifact is sound evidence for outcomes 1, 2, and 6. It is not evidence for BSP.

An AI-prohibited island inside an AI-permitted course does not work

It fails on enforceability, because distinguishing assisted from unassisted work in a compressed asynchronous session needs proctoring the schedule cannot absorb. On coherence, because students would be told within one week to use AI for one assignment and not another, in a course whose premise is that AI assistance is normal, with the justification living in an accreditation document they never see. And on risk, because a policy that counterintuitive will leak, and the exposure is the credibility of the whole mapping.

The benefit runs the other way too

The unassisted foundation BSP requires is precisely the preparation ITSY 1377's outcome 6 assumes. A student who has personally written and debugged code is equipped to judge whether AI-generated code is correct. A student who has not is comparing one unfamiliar text against another and calling it evaluation.

3.5Conclusion, for a justification memo

ITSY 1377 covers 9 of the 18 topic elements in the NSA Basic Scripting and Programming knowledge unit, which is 50 percent against a required 75 percent, and it cannot demonstrate two of that unit's six required outcomes at all: automating system administration tasks, and regular expressions. Bringing the course to the required standard would take an estimated 26 additional instructional hours in a 64-hour course that has already cut content to fit a seven-week session, and every combination of cuts large enough to free that time destroys or materially reduces at least three of the course's own six required outcomes. There is also a problem that no amount of time fixes: ITSY 1377 permits AI assistance on all coding by design, because it has no programming prerequisite and because one of its outcomes requires students to evaluate AI-generated code, so nothing produced in the course can serve as evidence that a student personally wrote and executed a script, which is exactly what the knowledge unit requires. The Basic Scripting and Programming unit must therefore be carried by a course with a conventional programming pedagogy and a conventional AI policy.

04Mapping against the proposed 2027-2028 plan

The plan changed substantially, and the analysis changes with it. What follows replaces the earlier mapping.

4.1What the new plan does well

Two of the three hardest gaps close outright

The earlier analysis flagged AI Governance and AI Fundamentals as units with no home anywhere in the catalog. The new plan creates purpose-built carriers for both: ITSY 2373 IT Governance, Regulation, and Compliance for AIG, which is the easiest win in the plan, and ITSY 1371 AI Fundamentals for AIF. Both are Local Need courses, so the college authors their outcomes.

The credit-hour problem is also solved. The plan totals exactly 60 SCH; COSC 1436 and the two-course Cisco sequence came out to make room. And ITSC 2325 is now required rather than an OR-option, which matters because an OR-option can never carry a knowledge unit.

4.2What the new plan breaks

There is no longer any programming or scripting course in the degree. COSC 1436 is gone and no DSAI course is present, so both carriers the previous analysis relied on are unavailable. That puts Basic Scripting and Programming back in jeopardy, and this time the Linux sequence cannot rescue it.

4.3The Linux sequence cannot carry BSP, and the reason is precise

The decisive finding

Across all eight WECM end-of-course outcomes for ITSC 1316 and ITSC 2325 combined, there is not one verb about writing, executing, or debugging a script. No "write," no "script," no "automate." Neither course's WECM description mentions scripting.

CourseWECM end-of-course outcomes, verbatim
ITSC 1316
Linux Installation and Configuration		(1) Install, administer, and manage a Linux system. (2) Demonstrate proficiency with Linux utilities, commands, and applications. (3) Identify and resolve security-based issues. (4) Integrate a Linux system into an existing network.
ITSC 2325
Advanced Linux		(1) Install, administer, and manage advanced network environment using a Linux system. (2) Demonstrate advanced skills and proficiency with Linux utilities and configurations. (3) Deploy secure networks. (4) Integrate Linux networks with existing networks.

Shell scripting, regular expressions, permissions, and cron are taught in these courses at most colleges. Blinn's ITSC 1316 schedule lists "Scripting" in weeks 13 and 14; the standard Eckert textbook has a "Shell Scripts" section; Laredo builds ITSC 2325 on Red Hat RH134, which teaches writing bash scripts. But none of it is in the state record. It is local syllabus content, which means a different instructor can drop all of it and remain fully WECM-compliant.

That matters because the WECM Protocol Manual designates end-of-course outcomes as the "heart" of a course and the minimum "meant to be taught and measured." Resting a required knowledge unit on content below that line is exactly the weak position a reviewer probes.

Two BSP items are not covered even locally: input and parameter validation, and script debugging. Neither could be verified in any ITSC 1316 or ITSC 2325 syllabus.

And the Windows half is a confirmed negative. Neither ITNW 1308 nor ITNW 2405 covers PowerShell or authored Windows scripting at outcome level. ITNW 1308's five WECM outcomes never mention scripting or automation; the closest ITNW 2405 outcome is "Utilize various operating system utilities and commands," which is command usage, not authored scripting. BSP element 6 requires both platforms explicitly.

4.4The fix already exists as a statewide WECM course

ITSC 1342 Shell Programming: Scripting (3 SCH, statewide)

Its WECM description reads: "Reading, writing, and debugging shell scripts. Development of scripts to automate frequently executed sequences of commands. Covers conditional logic, user interaction, loops, and menus to enhance the productivity and effectiveness of the user."

That single description puts authored scripting, debugging, automation of repeated command sequences, conditional logic, and loops into the state record. It is close to a purpose-written answer to BSP Outcome 1.

Two adjacent statewide options if a different number suits: ITSC 1307 UNIX Operating System I, whose description includes "and writing script files," and ITSC 2337 UNIX Operating System II, whose description includes "scripting languages."

05The capacity collision, which is the real blocker

The plan as written fails validation, and not for the reason it looks like.

With optimal local authoring the plan carries 10 of 11 units. That still fails, because the unit it drops sits inside a block where all units are required. And the failure is not a content gap. It is a capacity collision: ITSY 1371 is structurally required to be two courses at once.

If ITSY 1371 must carry BSP, because nothing else can, it has to teach Python from zero. That costs four to five weeks, which displaces the machine learning content it also needs to carry.

ITSY 1371 configurationAIFMLFVerdict
AIF + MLF + Python for BSP7 of 710 of 14, 71%MLF fails. Python displaces neural networks, NLP, and generative AI
AIF + MLF, no Python7 of 711 of 14, 79%Passes, but single-carrier and one disagreement from failure
ITSY 1371 (classical) + ITSY 1377 (modern half)7 of 714 of 14, 100%Passes robustly, two carriers

The contact-hour arithmetic is blunt: AIF's seven topic areas take roughly five weeks, classical machine learning another five, neural networks two, and Python from zero another four to five. That is 16 to 17 weeks of new content in a 16-week term with no room for assessment. It does not fit at any lecture and lab coding, because the lab hours are already consumed by the machine learning work.

So BSP must leave ITSY 1371

That single move is what unlocks everything else in the plan.

06The single smallest change

The recommendation

Replace ITSC 2325 Advanced Linux (3 SCH) with ITSC 1342 Shell Programming: Scripting (3 SCH) in the Advanced Core.

  • Zero credit-hour cost. Three out, three in. The plan stays at 60. Nothing else moves.
  • No THECB Local Need approval. ITSC 1342 is a statewide WECM course, so adding it is an inventory action.
  • It puts BSP evidence into the state record, rather than a local syllabus a reviewer must take on faith and a future instructor can silently drop.
  • It costs almost nothing pedagogically. ITSC 2325's four outcomes carry no knowledge unit that is not already carried elsewhere: its Linux network administration content overlaps ITNW 2405 and ITSY 2301. It is the least unit-productive course in the plan.
  • It unlocks Machine Learning Fundamentals, by removing the Python burden from ITSY 1371.

Second best, if the committee will not drop Advanced Linux: upgrade ITSY 1371 to a 4 SCH Local Need course and recover the hour by moving ITSY 2443 Forensics to a 3 SCH sibling. That costs a Local Need revision plus a second inventory change, and still leaves BSP resting entirely on locally authored text. Prefer the swap.

09Math and machine learning, specifically

9.1Math Fundamentals has two blockers

Blocker one: the slot cannot carry a unit at all. "Mathematics, 1 course, MATH 1314 given as example" means a student may satisfy the degree with MATH 1332, MATH 1342, or MATH 1350. A select-one slot carries nothing regardless of content, because carriers must be mandatory for every student. This is definitional.

Blocker two: even pinned, MATH 1314 fails on content.

No single Texas ACGM mathematics course covers both halves, because ACGM deliberately splits the algebra, statistics, and quantitative reasoning pathways. MATH 1314 and MATH 1342 are near-perfect complements, which is why the knowledge unit's own note says it is "likely covered by multiple math courses."

The fix costs zero credit hours: pin MATH 1314 by name rather than as an example; elect and document sequences and series in the local MATH 1314 syllabus, which the ACGM explicitly permits; and write descriptive statistics, probability, and elementary inferential methods into ITSY 1371. That reaches 7 of 7, or 6 of 7 if a reviewer discounts inferential statistics taught in a non-mathematics course. Either clears 75 percent.

One institutional check, and it is the only place a unit could be genuinely lost

Confirm that a program may narrow a core curriculum slot to a single named course. Most Texas institutions permit this; some forbid programs from restricting core options. If MATH 1314 cannot be pinned, MAF cannot be carried, because there is no in-major substitute for function composition and inverses. That is one email to the core curriculum office.

9.2Machine Learning Fundamentals needs the split

Fourteen topics, so eleven are required. ITSY 1371 reaches eleven only if it is not also teaching Python. Adding ITSY 1377's transformer, NLP, and generative AI content brings it to fourteen of fourteen with two carriers.

This is not a paper split. ITSY 1377 genuinely cannot teach agentic AI without transformers, embeddings, prompt engineering, and fine-tuning. The two courses cover complementary halves.

Four items to resolve before submission

1. A credit-hour discrepancy that will be caught

The plan lists ITSY 2430 Intrusion Detection at 3 SCH, but under WECM numbering the second digit is the credit value, so 2430 denotes 4 SCH. Every other course in the plan is internally consistent. The stated Advanced Core total of 22 and grand total of 60 hold only if it is 3 SCH. If the number is right, the plan is 61 SCH and exceeds the cap. If the credit value is right, the number should be ITSY 2330, the 3 SCH intrusion detection course in the statewide inventory. This does not affect any unit mapping, but it must be reconciled.

2. A course number collision

ITSY 1371 is already in use in Texas under other titles, including at Collin College ("OSINT and Introductory Security Analysis"). Local Need numbers are institution-scoped so this does not block the college, but it will collide in any articulation or transfer discussion. Consider a different number in the 1370 to 1379 band. ITSY 2373 appears unclaimed, though that cannot be proven statewide because the THECB Local Need registry search is retired.

3. A networking gap worth documenting

Subnetting and the TCP/IP model are absent from ITNW 1425's statewide description and from both published outcome lists. Del Mar College's local description adds "protocols and subnetting," which shows colleges do this locally. Document a local addendum with graded lab artifacts. Note also that Del Mar's catalog states ITNW 1425 "does not fulfill CCNA requirements," so do not claim anything the Cisco sequence carried beyond Basic Networking.

4. A sourcing caveat for the package

No WECM or ACGM outcome quoted in this analysis comes from a THECB-published source. The THECB WECM interactive search application is retired: its form target returns HTTP 404 and the successor host does not resolve. Every outcome here is transcribed from college master syllabi, strongest where El Paso Community College publishes them under an explicit heading reading "THECB Learning Outcomes (WECM)," corroborated across six or more colleges with near-exact agreement.

Three items to confirm with the curriculum office: ITNW 1425's authoritative outcomes (two colleges publish five-item lists sharing almost no wording, and "basic troubleshooting" appears in only one); ITSC 1342's end-of-course outcomes (the description is confirmed and is doing the heavy lifting for BSP, but the outcomes were not retrievable); and the two Local Need number questions above.
