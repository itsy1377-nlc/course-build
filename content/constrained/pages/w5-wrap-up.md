::: learned | What You Learned
1. An alert is a claim. It answers one narrow question and stops there.
2. A timeline row with no record behind it is not evidence.
3. Every row gets a mark: accepted, corrected, or rejected.
4. A confident sentence from a person fails the same test as one from a model.
5. Intent and severity are not in the pack, and saying so is part of the call.
:::

::: did | What You Did
1. Scoped an assistant to read-only access over one evidence pack.
2. Asked for a timeline that named a record on every row.
3. Opened every record the timeline named and marked each row.
4. Found at least one claim the record did not support.
5. Made the call, named the control you would build, and handed it over.
:::

::: checkpoint | Check Yourself
1. Your timeline has a row with a time, an actor, and no record. What do you do
   with it?
2. The assistant only writes a timeline. Why does read-only access still matter?
3. A classmate writes: "the assistant verified that no customer data left." What
   is wrong with that sentence?
4. You are sure the pack shows an incident, but you cannot point at one record
   that decides it. Have you made the call?
5. Someone asks you how bad it was. What do you tell them?

Answers:

1. Mark it rejected. A claim you cannot open is not evidence, whatever else the
   row holds.
2. Because the grant is the risk. An assistant that could write or send might
   change the evidence you are about to check, and you would not know.
3. It cites the assistant. "Verified" is the assistant's word for its own
   output. Only a record verifies a claim.
4. No. A call is the decision plus the record it rests on. Without the record
   you have an opinion.
5. That severity is not in the pack. Say what the records show, say what they do
   not, and name what you would need to rate it.
:::
