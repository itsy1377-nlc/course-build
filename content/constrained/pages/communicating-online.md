::: intro
This course runs online with no required class meetings. Almost all contact is
written. This page says where each kind of message goes, and how to write one
that gets a useful answer.
:::

::: section | Two places, and they do different jobs
| Place | Use it for | Who reads it |
|---|---|---|
| Questions and Answers board | Setup, errors, what a step means, anything about the work | The whole class |
| Canvas Inbox | Grades, extensions, health, anything personal | Your instructor |

One test decides which to use. If the answer would help another student, it
belongs on the board.
:::

::: section | How to ask so you get an answer
Most questions that stall are missing one of these five things.

1. What you were trying to do.
2. The week and the step you are on.
3. The exact command you ran.
4. The exact error text, copied.
5. What you already tried.

**Copy the text of an error. Never post a picture of your screen.** Nobody can
search a photograph, and half of it is usually cut off.
:::

::: callout tip | The two hour rule
If one step passes two hours, stop and post. Being stuck is normal here, and it
is not a sign that you do not belong. Post your question, then work on another
part of the week while you wait.
:::

::: section | Answering each other is welcome
You may answer a classmate, and doing it well teaches you more than reading. Point
at the step, the page, or the record that helped you.

Do not paste your finished answer to graded work. That helps nobody, and it is an
integrity problem for both of you.
:::

::: section | Never post these
- Real personal data, yours or anyone else's.
- Passwords, card numbers, or account numbers.
- Your full answer to a graded assignment.
- Another student's name, work, or grade.
- Anything aimed at a system outside your three permitted targets.
:::

::: section | Write it the way you would write it at work
Your classmates are adults of many ages, and English is a second language for
some of them. Plain sentences serve everyone.

Skip sarcasm, jokes about people, and slang. Assume the other person means well.
If a post reads badly to you, ask what they meant before you answer it.
:::
