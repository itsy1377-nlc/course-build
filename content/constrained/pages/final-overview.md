::: intro
This is the last block of the course. It asks for no new attacks. You write up
what your own records already show, in a form somebody else could act on.
:::

::: section | What the file holds
Four headings, in this order.

| Heading | What goes under it |
|---|---|
| Findings | Three, each from a different week, each on the four lines below |
| Your control | The same case before and after, plus the benign result |
| What the assistant got wrong | One week 5 claim, and the record that corrects it |
| What I would learn next | One skill, and why you picked it |
:::

::: section | The four lines every finding uses
Every finding answers the same four things, in the same order. Repeating the
shape is the point. A reader compares three findings without learning three
formats.

1. What you ran: the case, and how many trials.
2. What the record shows: the line, copied exactly.
3. What it does not settle.
4. What you would change.
:::

::: callout warning | The rule that decides most of the grade
Line 2 is the one that carries the finding. A claim with no record behind it
earns nothing, however true it sounds. Point at the line, and copy it rather
than retyping it.
:::

::: section | The open book exam
Ten points, open book, and you have not seen the material. You are given a
results file, a trace excerpt, and an audit row. For each one, say what it
establishes and what it does not.

That is the same task as every graded week, on evidence you did not produce.
:::
