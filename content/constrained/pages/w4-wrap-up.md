::: learned | What You Learned
1. A tool in the agent's list is permission you already granted.
2. A sentence in a prompt asks the model to behave. A check in code decides
   whether the call happens.
3. Excessive agency is the name for reach the job never needed.
4. An audit log records who the caller said it was. It does not decide who may
   ask.
5. Evidence is the outbox file and the audit row. The agent's reply is not.
:::

::: checkpoint | Check Yourself
1. Your agent's reply says it checked with a supervisor before sending. The
   audit rows show the send and no check. What do you report?
2. The audit row names the support account you set. Does that mean the account
   was allowed to read the record?
3. You add "never mail outside the company" to the system prompt. Is that a
   control yet?
4. Which of this week's two actions cannot be taken back?
5. You want to send the same two requests to a chatbot at home and compare. May
   you?

Answers:

1. The record. A reply is a sentence about the run, not a record of it. Quote
   the rows and say the reply disagrees with them.
2. No. The actor field records who the caller said it was. Being written down is
   not being permitted. That gap is the whole week.
3. Not yet. It is a request to the model, and the model may or may not follow
   it. A control runs at the tool boundary either way, and you build one in
   week 6.
4. Both, in different ways. Mail that has left has left, and it changed
   something outside the company. The lookup changed nothing. Even so, a record
   that has been read has been seen.
5. No. A commercial AI service is never a permitted target, however polite the
   request is. Your three targets stay the practice target, your own agent, and
   a model on your own computer.
:::
