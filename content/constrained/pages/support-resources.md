::: intro
Nobody finishes a course alone. This page says who handles what, so you spend
your time on the work instead of on finding the right door.
:::

::: section | Course help, in order
1. **The Questions and Answers board.** Anything about the lab, the lesson, or
   an error. Post the exact error text. Your classmates are stuck on the same
   things, and an answer there helps all of them.
2. **The Canvas Inbox.** Grades, deadlines, accommodations, or anything you
   would rather not post. Your instructor reads it.
3. **The week 1 setup walkthrough.** Check it first when a command fails. Most
   setup problems are one missing step.

Say what you tried and what happened. "It does not work" cannot be answered.
:::

::: section | College services
| Office | Ask them about |
|---|---|
| Disability Support Services | Accommodations, and the letter that arranges them |
| Advising | Your degree plan, and what to take next |
| Tutoring services | Reading, writing, study habits, and Python basics |
| The library | Research help, study space, and access to sources |
| Counseling services | Stress, and anything getting in the way of school |
| Financial aid | Cost, aid, and enrolment questions |

Each office has a link in the Canvas sidebar. If you cannot find one, ask your
instructor and you will be pointed to it.
:::

::: section | Accommodations
Arrange accommodations through Disability Support Services. They send the
letter, and your instructor follows it.

Tell your instructor early in the term, not the week something is due. If a
course page or a lab step is hard to use with your assistive tool, say so. That
is a fault in the page and it gets fixed.
:::

::: section | This course costs nothing
There is no textbook to buy. There is no software licence, no cloud account, and
no paid service. Everything runs on your own computer.

If your computer cannot run the local model, message your instructor early.
There is a supported path built into the course for exactly that, and it does
not cost you anything.
:::

::: section | If the code is the hard part
This course has no programming prerequisite, and most students here have not
written Python before. Reading a short script is a skill you build during the
term, not one you arrive with.

The Course Material page lists free ways to practise Python. Tutoring services
can also sit with you and read a script line by line.
:::

::: callout note | If you are a dual credit student
Your high school counselor is part of your support too. They handle schedules,
testing arrangements, and anything your high school has to approve. College
offices still serve you, and so does your instructor.
:::

::: section | When life gets in the way
Illness, work, and family emergencies happen to everyone in this course. Message
your instructor before the deadline if you can, and after it if you could not.

The rule on late work is on the Course Policies page. Read it now, while nothing
has gone wrong.
:::
