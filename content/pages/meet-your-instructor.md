::: intro
This page says who teaches this course, how to reach that person, and how fast
you can expect an answer. Read it before you send your first message.
:::

::: section | Your instructor
Your instructor's name, photo, and contact details appear in the Canvas course
navigation under Instructor Information.

This course is taught by a working practitioner in cyber defense. The role here
is not to lecture at you. Nothing meets live, so your instructor spends that
time on three things instead:

1. Answering questions on the Q and A Discussion Board, where the whole class
   can read the answer.
2. Reading your findings report and your walkthrough, and telling you what a
   client would say about them.
3. Watching the gradebook for students who have gone quiet, and reaching out
   before it is too late to fix.

Most of your work is scored by an autograder, which is fast and never tired.
Your instructor grades the parts a machine cannot judge: whether your finding is
accurate, and whether your fix is adequate.
:::

::: section | How to reach your instructor
Use the **Canvas Inbox**. It is the message tool in the Canvas global
navigation menu on the left.

Your ACES student email also works. Nothing else does. A message sent from a
personal email account, or by text, may not reach your instructor at all.

Put the course name in your subject line. Say what you tried and what happened.
That saves a round trip.

Before you send a private message, ask yourself one question: would another
student benefit from this answer? If yes, post it on the Q and A board instead.
:::

::: callout time | How fast you get a reply
**Q and A Discussion Board: within one business day.** This board is checked
every weekday. It is the fastest way to get an answer.

**Canvas Inbox and email: within two business days.** Weekends and college
holidays do not count.

**Grades and feedback: within one week of the due date.** Autograded work
scores right away, so you usually know your result the moment you submit.

If two business days pass with no reply, send the message again. Messages do go
missing, and nobody is annoyed by a second try.
:::

::: section | Office hours
Office hours are held over Zoom, by appointment.

To book one, send a Canvas Inbox message with two or three times that work for
you. You get back a Zoom link and a confirmed time.

There is no standing weekly meeting. This course is fully asynchronous, and a
fixed hour would only serve the students free at that hour. An appointment
serves everyone.

Come to an appointment with the exact error text and what you have already
tried. Fifteen focused minutes usually settles a problem that a week of typing
did not.
:::

::: section | What your instructor expects from you
1. **Start each week early.** Most problems here are setup problems, and setup
   problems take a day to solve, not an hour.
2. **Ask before you are stuck for four days.** Being stuck is normal. Staying
   stuck and silent is what ends courses.
3. **Show your work when you ask.** Paste the command, the error, and what you
   expected. Do not describe it from memory.
4. **Read the Rules of Engagement and follow them.** That page is the one place
   where a mistake has consequences outside the gradebook.
:::
