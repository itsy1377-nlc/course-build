::: intro
Most of your work here is done off campus with nobody watching, which is
exactly why this page exists.
:::

::: section | The seven kinds of violation
| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Cheating | Using help you were not allowed to use, or letting someone copy |
| Plagiarism | Using another author's words or ideas as your own |
| Collusion | Helping someone else be dishonest, or paying someone to do your work |
| Fabrication | Inventing a result, a number, or a source you never read |
| Academic misconduct | Breaking college rules on purpose, such as changing a grade |
| Academic enticement | Offering an employee anything of value for a grade or a waiver |
| Sale of academic product | Selling or buying coursework. Tutoring and editing are fine |
:::

::: callout warning | Fabrication is the one to watch in this course
Security work is full of chances to invent a result. All four of these are
fabrication, and all four are graded as violations:

1. Reporting an attack you did not run.
2. Editing a result line before you paste it into a report.
3. Writing that a fix held when you never re-ran the test.
4. Citing a framework identifier you did not look up.

An honest negative result earns full credit. A made-up success is a violation.
:::

::: section | What you may share with classmates
| You may share | You may not share |
|---|---|
| How an attack works, and why | Your code for a graded module |
| What an error message means | Your report, or any part of it |
| Where to look in the code | Your results, evidence, or quiz answers |
| A payload, discussed on the board | Your API key, with anyone, ever |

Your lab holds fake secrets built from your own student ID. A classmate can hand
you an idea. They cannot hand you your proof.

Cite anything you did not write: what it is, where you found it, and which part
you used. The librarians help with any citation style.
:::

::: section | What happens after a violation
1. The work receives a **zero**.
2. The violation is documented with Northeast Lakeview College.
3. Depending on how serious it was, you can receive an F for the course.
4. A Rules of Engagement violation also goes to the Dean of Student Success.
:::
