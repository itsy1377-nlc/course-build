::: intro
This page is for the moment something technical breaks. It says who fixes what,
and it draws the line between a Canvas problem and a lab problem. Those two go
to different places.
:::

::: callout warning | Technical trouble does not move a deadline
Say it once so it is clear. A broken laptop, a dead router, or a slow upload
does not extend a due date.

So build in room. Submit a day early. Keep a backup way to get online, such as a
campus lab, a library, or a hotspot you can borrow from the college.

If a college system is down, that is different. Report it right away, keep the
ticket number, and message your instructor the same day.
:::

::: section | Canvas problems
A Canvas problem is one where Canvas itself misbehaves: a page will not load, a
quiz will not open, a file will not upload, your grades look wrong.

Canvas help is available every day, at any hour. Click **Help** in the gray
global navigation menu on the left of any Canvas page. From there you get:

1. **The Canvas Guides**, which are searchable and cover almost everything.
2. **Live chat** with Canvas support.
3. **A phone line.** Call the district help desk and choose the option
   announced for Canvas support. Menu options change, so listen rather than
   dialing from memory. The district IT support page lists the current numbers
   and hours.
4. **A direct link to message your instructor.**

If you have never used Canvas, open the **Student Quick Guide** in that same
left menu first. It is a short walkthrough written for new students.
:::

::: section | Login, password, and hardware problems
The district help desk handles the things that are not Canvas.

They can help with:

- An ACES password reset.
- Browser trouble and pop-up blocker settings.
- Wi-Fi and low-cost internet options.
- Zoom problems.
- Checking out a laptop or a hotspot.
- General troubleshooting when you do not know what is wrong.

Current phone numbers, the email address, and hours of operation are on the
Alamo Colleges District IT support page. Check there rather than trusting a
number copied from an old course.

Have this ready before you call. It makes the call short:

1. Your ACES ID.
2. Your Banner ID.
3. Your date of birth.

Give only what the help desk asks for. Nobody from the college will ask for
your password.
:::

No phone number or web address is printed on this page, and that is deliberate.

and nobody notices.

or what you type into a search box.

Each search term above is the name of an office or a page. Look for the result
named for it. If you land somewhere else, add Alamo Colleges to your search.
:::

::: callout note | Where the direct links live
Your instructor puts a direct link to each item in that table into the Canvas
syllabus, and checks those links at the start of each session.

They live there rather than here because this page is copied forward from
session to session without being reopened. The syllabus is rewritten for each
session, so it is the one place a link gets checked again.

Open **Syllabus** in the course navigation on the left. If a link there is
dead, say so on the Q and A board.
:::

::: section | Borrowing a laptop or a hotspot
Each of the five Alamo colleges runs its own help desk, and each one lends
equipment.

If you need a loaner laptop or an internet hotspot, contact the Northeast
Lakeview College help desk and ask whether you qualify. These are free, and
they are usually available for immediate checkout.

Do this in week one if your setup is shaky. Do not wait until week six to find
out your computer cannot run the labs.
:::

::: section | Lab problems are not help desk problems
This is the most useful thing on this page.

The help desk supports Canvas, ACES, and college hardware. **Nobody there has
seen this course's lab code.** They cannot help you when Python throws an error
or the harness reports nothing.

Send lab problems to the **Q and A Discussion Board** instead. That is where
your instructor and your classmates are, and it is where the answer already
exists half the time.

| What broke | Who fixes it |
|---|---|
| Canvas page, quiz, or upload | Canvas support, through the Help menu |
| ACES login or password | District help desk |
| Wi-Fi, browser, or borrowed hardware | District or college help desk |
| Python, the agent, the target app, the harness | Q and A Discussion Board |
| A grade or a deadline | Canvas Inbox to your instructor |
:::

::: section | What your computer needs
Canvas works best on a **laptop or a desktop**. The Canvas mobile app is fine
for checking a grade or reading a page. Do not try to do lab work on a phone.

Keep your browser up to date. Microsoft Edge, Apple Safari, Google Chrome, and
Mozilla Firefox all work with Canvas.

For the labs you also need Python 3.11 or newer. You do not need administrator
rights to install it. The Course Material page covers the rest of the setup.
:::

::: section | How to report a problem so it gets fixed fast
Whether you are writing to the help desk or posting on the board, include these
five things:

1. What you were trying to do.
2. The exact steps you took.
3. What you expected to happen.
4. What actually happened, with the **exact error text** copied and pasted.
5. Your browser and operating system, or your Python version for a lab problem.

Do not describe an error from memory, and do not summarize it. Copy it. One
wrong word in a paraphrased error message can cost a day.

Never include your API key, and never post a screenshot that shows one.
:::

::: callout tip | Accessibility help built into Canvas
Canvas includes the Microsoft Immersive Reader. It can change the font, split
words into syllables, highlight a line at a time, and read a page aloud.

You turn it on once, and it then works in every course, because the setting
lives on your Canvas profile.

If you need more than that, contact Student Accessibility Services. The Support
Resources page in this course says how to reach them.
:::
