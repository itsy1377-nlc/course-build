::: intro
This is the last block of the course. It runs day 50 to day 55, Monday to
Saturday. There is no new content in it. You write your report, take the exam,
and record a short walkthrough of your own work.
:::

::: objectives
By the end of this block, you will be able to:

- F.1. Write a security finding a reader outside this course could act on.
- F.2. Specify a second fix, with the test that would prove it works.
- F.3. Name the weakness class in an agent setup you have never run.
- F.4. Explain your own repository out loud in three minutes.
:::

::: scope
Everything here still runs against HelpDesk Lite on your own computer. Nothing
else. The Rules of Engagement do not relax in the final block.

Your report has to state the authorization basis for every finding in it. That
is a graded row, not a formality.
:::

::: callout time | About how long this takes
The report: about 4 hours, including one fresh harness run.
The exam: 3 hours at most, and the timer is hard.
The walkthrough: about 90 minutes, including one practice take.

That is roughly 9 hours across six days. This block is lighter than a normal
week on purpose.
:::

::: callout stop | There are no incompletes and no make-up work
The term ends on day 55. There is no room after the exam to repair anything.

So every grade protection in this course is front-loaded. You already have all
of it:

1. Unlimited retakes on the Module 0 Gate.
2. Your lowest lab score is dropped.
3. Fallback branches your instructor published for every scaffold.
4. A 90 percent late window on Project 2 Part A, open day 50, closed day 51.

Use them now. Nothing new opens later.
:::

::: section | If you are behind, you still finish
Being behind is not the same as failing. Here is the whole path.

1. On Monday, day 50, pull the `harness-working` branch your instructor
   published.
2. Run it against your target and collect real results.
3. Do the analysis, the remediation, and the exam against that branch.

Working from a fallback branch caps that artifact at 80 percent. It is never a
zero. It never blocks you from the next step. A capped grade beats a missing
one.

Say in your report that you used it. Using a published fallback is honest work.
Hiding it is not.
:::
