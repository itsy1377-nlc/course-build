::: intro
This board is where you ask anything about the course, the labs, or an error you
cannot get past. It is not graded, and it stays pinned to the top of the
discussions list all term. Bookmark it now.
:::

::: section | What this board is for
Post here when your question is about the course itself rather than about you.

Good things to post:

1. An error you cannot get past.
2. A command that does not do what the page said it would.
3. A part of a reading you did not follow.
4. A question about how an assignment will be graded.
5. Something you figured out that other people will hit too.

Your instructor checks this board every weekday and answers within one business
day. Classmates often answer sooner.

**Ask here before you send a private message.** If the answer helps you, it
helps ten other people, and they can find it later.
:::

::: callout stop | Never post an API key
Your API key is a credential. So is a password, and so is a token.

1. Never paste a key into a post, a reply, or a code block.
2. **Never post a screenshot that contains one.** A key is easy to miss in a
   terminal window, in an editor tab, or in a settings file.
3. Before you attach an image, look at every line of it.

If a key does end up here, act the same minute. Turn it off at the provider,
make a new one, and message your instructor. Nobody is in trouble for reporting
a leak quickly.
:::

::: directions
A good question gets solved in one reply. A vague one costs four days. Include
these four things, in this order.

**1. What you were trying to do.**
One sentence. "I was running the harness against case A2."

**2. What you actually ran.**
The exact command, in a code block, copied from your terminal.

```
python -m harness.runner --case A2-indirect-injection-ticket
```

**3. What you expected to happen.**
This matters more than people think. Half of all questions here turn out to be
a correct result that looked wrong.

**4. What happened instead.**
The **exact error text**, copied and pasted. Not a summary, and not a
description from memory.

```
ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'harness'
```

Then add your operating system and your Python version. Two lines of context
often replace a whole day of back and forth.

**One more thing that helps everyone.** Give your post a title that says the
problem. "Harness says no module named harness" is findable. "Help please" is
not.
:::

::: section | How to answer someone else
Answering is the fastest way to learn this material, and you are encouraged to
do it.

- **Point, do not paste.** Name the file and the idea. Do not hand over your
  code for a graded module.
- **Say how you know.** "I hit this too, and it was my virtual environment"
  helps more than a bare command.
- **Be kind about basics.** Everyone here started somewhere, and half the class
  has never opened a terminal before this course.

The Academic Integrity page draws the line. You may share how something works.
You may not share your code, your results, or your quiz answers.
:::

::: section | What does not belong here
Four kinds of post go somewhere else.

1. **Anything private.** A grade, a deadline request, a personal situation. Use
   the Canvas Inbox.
2. **A credential.** See the warning above.
3. **Anything about a system outside your lab.** No attacks, no findings, and no
   questions about anything the Rules of Engagement do not authorize.
4. **A Canvas or password problem.** Those go to the help desk. The Canvas and
   Tech Support page says how.
:::
