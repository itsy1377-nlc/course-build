::: scope
Everything you post comes from your own run against the lab target on your own

Do not post your API key, and do not post a screenshot with one in it.
:::

::: section | Why this one exists
Findings get read. Somebody senior reads yours before it goes to a client, and
you read a teammate's before it goes anywhere.

The question is never whether it sounds right. It is whether the evidence
supports what the finding says, and only what it says.
:::

::: directions
**Part 1: post one finding.** Due Thursday, so people have something to read.

Pick one case from your own harness run. Post:

1. The case ID and the exact result line, copied, including the rate.
2. Your evidence: the outbox file, the audit row, or the canary match. Paste
   it. Do not describe it.
3. One sentence saying what the evidence proves.
4. One sentence saying what it does **not** prove.

Item 4 is the hard one. A message that left the company proves a message left
the company. It does not prove what was inside it.

Keep it under 200 words.

**Part 2: reply to one classmate.** Due Sunday.

Pick a post nobody has replied to yet. In two or three sentences, answer this:

> Would that evidence convince you? Say why, or why not.

Useful things to notice:

- Does the evidence show what happened, or what the model said happened?
- Does the claim reach further than the evidence does?
- Is the rate given, or is it just "vulnerable"?
- Could you reproduce this from what they wrote?

**Disagreeing is the point.** A reply that says "looks good" is worth nothing.
If the evidence does not support the claim, say so, say which part, and say
what would fix it. Keep it about the finding, never about the person.
:::

::: callout tip | If somebody says your evidence does not hold
They are probably right, and this is the cheapest place in your career to find
that out. Reply, say what you would change, and move on.
:::

::: grading
Complete or incomplete, 15 points.

Full credit is a post with all four parts and a reply that answers the
question. A reply that only agrees gets nothing.
:::
