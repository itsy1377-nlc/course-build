::: section | Why this one exists
You have spent six weeks learning to grade the world instead of the prose. You
know that what a model said is not evidence, and that a file in the outbox is.

None of that is worth much until you use it on somebody else's claim.

In a real job, findings get read. Somebody senior reads yours before it goes to
a client. You read a teammate's before it goes anywhere. The question they ask
is never "does this sound right." It is "does the evidence here support what
this says, and only what this says."

That question is the whole assignment.
:::

::: directions
**Part 1: post one finding.** Due Thursday, so people have something to read.

Pick one case from your own harness run. Post:

1. The case ID and the exact result line, copied, including the rate.
2. Your evidence. The outbox file, the audit row, or the canary match. Paste
   it. Do not describe it.
3. One sentence saying what the evidence proves.
4. One sentence saying what it does **not** prove.

Item 4 is the hard one and it is worth reading twice. A message that left the
company proves a message left the company. It does not prove what was inside
it. Being exact about the difference is most of this skill.

Keep it under 200 words. Short and exact beats long and vague.

**Part 2: reply to one classmate.** Due Sunday.

Pick a post nobody has replied to yet. Answer one question:

> Would that evidence convince you? Say why, or why not.

Two or three sentences. You are looking at one thing: does the evidence
support the claim, and only the claim? Useful things to notice:

- Does the evidence show what happened, or what the model said happened?
- Does the claim reach further than the evidence does?
- Is the rate given, or is it just "vulnerable"?
- Would you be able to reproduce this from what they wrote?

**Disagreeing is the point.** A reply that says "looks good" is worth nothing
to the person who wrote it. If the evidence does not support the claim, say so,
say which part, and say what would fix it. Do it the way you would want it done
to you: about the finding, never about the person.
:::

::: callout tip | If somebody says your evidence does not hold
They are probably right, and this is the cheapest place in your career to find
that out. Reply, say what you would change, and move on. Nobody is graded on
being right the first time.
:::

::: grading
Complete or incomplete, 15 points.

You get full credit for a post that has all four parts and a reply that
actually answers the question. That is the entire bar.

You do not get credit for a reply that only agrees.
:::
