::: intro
Most of your work here happens with nobody watching. This page says what counts
as your own work, and what follows when it is not.
:::

::: section | The kinds of violation
| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Cheating | Using help you were not allowed to use |
| Plagiarism | Passing off another writer's words or ideas as yours |
| Collusion | Helping somebody else be dishonest |
| Fabrication | Inventing a result, a number, or a source |
| Misconduct | Breaking a college rule on purpose |

The district procedure is the authority here, not this page. Open it from the
Canvas sidebar and read it once.
:::

::: callout warning | Fabrication is the one that catches people
Security work is full of chances to invent a result. All four of these count:

1. Reporting a run you did not make.
2. Editing a record line before you paste it.
3. Writing that a control held without running it again.
4. Citing a framework identifier you did not look up.

An honest result that shows nothing earns full credit here. Every rubric in this
course says so, in writing.
:::

::: section | What you may share, and what you may not
| Share this | Never share this |
|---|---|
| How an attack works | Your write-up, or any part of it |
| What an error message means | Your records, counts, or grids |
| Where to look in the code | Your gateway token, with anyone, ever |

A classmate can hand you an idea. They cannot hand you your evidence, because
your evidence carries your token. Clause 3 of the Rules of Engagement page
covers tokens.
:::

::: section | What happens after a violation
1. The work receives a zero.
2. It is documented, and a repeat can mean an F.
3. A Rules of Engagement violation also goes to the Dean of Student Success.

Being unsure is not a violation. Ask on the Questions and Answers board, or
message your instructor in the Canvas Inbox.
:::
