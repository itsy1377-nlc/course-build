::: learned | What You Learned
1. Python keeps data and instructions apart. A model keeps them in one stream.
2. Words in a ticket can act like an order, because nothing marks them as data.
3. A hit is a record your tool left behind, not a sentence the model wrote.
4. A refusal and a failure are different events, and mixing them hides both.
5. A control that never fires bounds what your other grid is allowed to mean.
:::

::: did | What You Did
1. Wrote ticket text aimed at a tool your own week 1 record marked irreversible.
2. Ran thirty clean trials in the document arm and watched the grid fill.
3. Ran the same instruction from your own message, thirty clean trials again.
4. Labelled every trial injected, failed, refused or errored, from the record.
5. Reported both rates with their clean-trial counts, and wrote a bounded claim.
:::

::: checkpoint | Check Yourself
1. Your document grid shows zero hits and your control grid shows nineteen.
   What may you say?
2. Your document grid shows zero hits and your control grid shows zero. What may
   you say?
3. A classmate reports "three out of thirty" and nothing else. What is missing?
4. Why is the refused label weaker evidence than the injected label?
5. Halfway through an arm you improve the ticket text. What have you done to
   your grid?
6. Why does the control go in your own message rather than in a second ticket?
7. Your ticket needs a customer name. Where does that name come from?

Answers:

1. That in these trials, on this model, the instruction fired from your own
   message and not from the ticket. That is all. It does not show your setup
   would have caught the same behaviour arriving from a ticket.
2. Very little. The instrument was not shown to work, so the document grid
   cannot be read. Saying that clearly is the full-credit answer.
3. The number of clean trials it rests on, and how many errored trials were
   re-run to get there.
4. Injected rests on a record the world kept. Refused rests on what the model
   wrote, and a model's words are not a report of its reasons.
5. Ended one arm and started another. Thirty trials of one payload is a grid;
   fifteen of each is two half-measurements.
6. Because the point is to send it from a position the model trusts. A second
   ticket is the same untrusted position, so it would test nothing new.
7. You invent it. Nothing real goes into the practice target, and the gateway
   keeps a record of everything you send.
:::
