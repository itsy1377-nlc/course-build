::: intro
This course is built around AI tools, so banning them would be strange. This
page says what you may use, what you have to declare, and what the gateway
record already knows.
:::

::: section | The part you should hear in week 1
The college gateway keeps its own record of every model call you make. The
gateway writes it, not your notebook. Nothing on your side changes it.

So a report about a run that never happened matches nothing. A grid of thirty
trials sits beside a gateway record showing no traffic that evening, and the two
do not line up.

You are being told this now, on purpose. A control students find out about in
week 6 feels like a trap, and a control nobody knows about stops nobody.
:::

::: callout note | What that record covers
It covers calls that went through the gateway. If your instructor declares an
outage and you work against the offline model, your notebook stamps that report
and the stamp stays on it.

The What Is Logged page in Start Here says what is kept, who may read it, and
for how long. Read that one properly.
:::

::: section | Why AI is permitted here
One outcome of this course is to check AI output against evidence. That outcome
is graded in week 6, where you find the wrong sentence in a handover note an AI
wrote.

To grade that, you have to see AI output and then judge it. A course that bans
AI cannot assess the skill. It can only claim it.
:::

::: section | The four conditions
**1. Declare it where you are asked.** Name the tool and say what you used it
for. One or two sentences is enough.

**2. Never paste your token into a model.** Not into an assistant, not into a
chat window, not into a file you share.

**3. Never paste personal information into any model.** Not yours, not a
classmate's, not a customer's from your job.

**4. You own what you submit.** "The model wrote it" is not a defence. If the
code is wrong, you are wrong. Every job in this field works that way.
:::

::: section | What is actually graded
Every graded claim points at a line in your own record. No assistant has seen
that file. Three real examples:

1. Which field on line 4 shows the arguments the model sent?
2. Your grid moved from 6 to 24. What does that support, and what does it not?
3. Which two records in your pack fail to reconcile?

AI is permitted, and on the questions that decide your grade it helps less than
you would think.
:::

::: callout warning | Where AI quietly costs you
Three failures show up every term:

1. **Invented functions** that belong to some other library.
2. **The missing error path.** The retry, the timeout and the bad reply get
   skipped.
3. **A confident wrong explanation** about the wrong file.

Run it. Read it. Then decide.
:::

::: section | Your writing is not scanned
Detectors get it wrong, and the accused student is then asked to prove a
negative. That lands hardest on students writing in a second language.

So this course removes the reason to cheat instead of policing the writing.
Dishonesty is still dishonesty. A classmate's work, an invented result, or an
undeclared AI use is handled under the Academic Integrity page.
:::
