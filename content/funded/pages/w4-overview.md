::: intro
This week you try to make your own agent call your own irreversible tool, using
words in a ticket. Then you check whether your test could have worked at all.
Both grids count, whichever way they fall.
:::

::: objectives
By the end of this week you will be able to:

1. Explain why text in a ticket can act like an instruction.
2. Label thirty trials in each arm from the record, not from the reply.
3. Say what a pair of grids does and does not establish, including a null.
:::

::: scope | Authorization and Scope
This week uses three of the permitted targets, and nothing else:

1. The practice target this course supplies.
2. The agent you built.
3. A model reached through the school gateway, using your own token.

Never an AI service you signed up for yourself. Never a system you do not own.
Never a classmate's notebook. Clause 1 of the Rules of Engagement page in Start
Here holds the full list. Put invented data in your ticket text, and nothing
else.
:::

Students new to programming often take two to three times as long on the parts
that involve code. That puts a realistic week between about 10 and 20 hours.
Sixty runs take real time to finish, so start on day 22, not on day 28. If a
run keeps failing, post on the Questions and Answers board with the error text.
