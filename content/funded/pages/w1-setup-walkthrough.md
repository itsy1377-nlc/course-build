::: intro
There is nothing to install for this course. The work runs on a college server
and you reach it with a browser. This page signs you in, shows you the two cells
that keep you safe, and tells you what your budget is.
:::

::: steps | Do this now
1. Pass the scope, safety and logging gate in Start Here. It is due day 3 and it
   gates the week 1 assignment.
2. Open the Canvas sidebar. Follow the link to the notebook server.
3. Sign in with your college account.
4. Wait for your notebook to start. The first start is the slow one.
5. Open the file named Week 1.
6. Run the first cell, the setup cell. Wait for it to print ready.
7. Read the lines it prints. Then run the check cell under it.
:::

::: section | What ready looks like
The setup cell prints a short block. Yours will look like this, with your own
alias and your own numbers.

```
setup: ready
notebook: week 1
token alias: student-7c4a
budget: shown in the bar above the filmstrip
tools registered: 1
```

If any line says something other than ready, run the check cell. It names the
problem in one sentence.
:::

::: section | Your notebook is yours alone
Every student gets a separate notebook on the same server. Nobody else can open
yours, and you cannot open theirs.

Files you save stay there between sessions. You can sign in from a college lab
machine, a laptop or a borrowed computer and find your work where you left it.
Nothing depends on the machine in front of you.
:::

::: section | The check cell
The check cell reads your current setup and tells you what is wrong. It says it
in one sentence you can act on.

It never calls the model, so running it costs nothing and you can run it as
often as you like. It knows the faults week 1 can produce: a tool that is not
registered, an empty tool list, a step limit of zero, and a token that has not
loaded.

**Run the check cell before you ask for help.** Most of the time it has already
answered the question.
:::

::: section | The reset cell
The reset cell puts week 1 back to a known good state. It restores the starter
function, the tool list, your settings including the step limit, and the
workspace folder. Then it draws an empty filmstrip.

It does not restart the notebook, and it takes a second or two.

So break things freely. This week asks you to break your tool six ways on
purpose, and the reset cell is what makes that safe. Run it between breaks so
each one starts from the same place.
:::

::: callout tip | Keep your written answers somewhere else
Write your assignment answers in your own document, not in the notebook. The
reset cell restores course code, and your notes should not depend on it.
:::

::: section | Your token and your budget
The gateway issues you a token. It is how the college knows a call came from
you, and how the cost lands in the right place. Your budget for the whole term
is under ten dollars.

Three rules, and they matter from day one:

1. Your token is yours. Do not share it, and do not use anyone else's.
2. The notebook shows your token by an alias, never in full. Keep it that way.
3. Watch the budget line. It is small on purpose.

Week 1 uses very little of it. Week 5 is where money becomes the lesson, and
that week issues you a separate key for the part meant to be spent.
:::

::: callout warning | Everything you type here is recorded
Every prompt you send and every reply that comes back is written down and kept
under college control. Your instructor can read it.

Do not paste a real password, a real card number, or anyone's personal
information into this course. The What Is Logged page in Start Here says what is
kept, who reads it, and for how long.
:::

::: callout note | If the gateway is not answering
Some days the model service will not be available. The notebook says so when
that happens, and your instructor will tell you what it means for the deadline.

That is not something you broke. Post on the Questions and Answers board if you
are unsure which one you are looking at.
:::

::: callout tip | If you get stuck
Post on the Questions and Answers board. Say what you ran, what the check cell
said, and copy the exact text of any error.

Copy the text. Do not retype it from memory, and do not post a photograph of
your screen. An error somebody can search for gets answered faster, and your
post helps the next person too.
:::

::: checkpoint | Check Yourself
1. Something in your notebook stopped working. Which cell do you run first?
2. You want the starter function back the way it came. Which cell?
3. A classmate cannot get their token to load and asks to borrow yours. What do
   you say?

Answers: (1) The check cell. It names the fault without calling the model. (2)
The reset cell. (3) No. Your token is yours alone, and every call it makes is
recorded against you. Send them to the Questions and Answers board.
:::
