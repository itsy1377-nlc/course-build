::: intro
Your instructor is a working practitioner in cyber defense. Most of this course
you do on your own time, so that time goes to the board, to your submissions,
and to students who go quiet.
:::

::: section | What a person grades
A person reads every submission, against the rubric on the assignment page.

One thing is weighed above the rest: whether the claim you wrote matches the
record you cited. A careful account of a result that went nowhere earns full
marks. A confident claim with nothing behind it earns none.
:::

::: section | Your instructor can read your prompts
The gateway records what you send and what comes back. Your instructor can open
it for two reasons: to see how the class is doing, and to settle a question
about one piece of work. A reason is written down each time.

The What Is Logged page in this module has the rest.
:::

::: steps | How to send a message that gets answered
1. **Ask whether other students need this answer.** If they do, post it on the
   Questions and Answers board instead.
2. **Say what you tried and what happened.** Paste the exact error text. Do not
   retype it from memory, and do not send a photograph of your screen.
3. **Wait two business days, then send it again.** Messages do go missing, and
   nobody minds a second try.
:::

::: section | What is expected of you
1. Start each week early. Access problems take a day to fix, not an hour.
2. Ask before you have been stuck for four days.
3. Follow the Rules of Engagement. That is the page where a mistake reaches past
   the gradebook.
:::
