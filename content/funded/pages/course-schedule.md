::: intro
Seven weeks, seven graded pieces of work, one hundred points. The whole schedule
is the first table on this page. Canvas carries the same deadlines on every
assignment.
:::

::: section | The days that are not a Sunday
Most work is due on a Sunday at 11:59 PM. Five deadlines are not.

| Day | What happens | Why it moves |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | The gate quiz is due | Week 1 does not open until you pass it |
| 17 | Your week 3 specification is due | The generator needs it before the session |
| 18 | The week 3 discovery session runs | It happens live, once |
| 45 | Your week 7 attack text is due | The board needs it before the rounds |
| 47 | The two week 7 rounds run | They happen live, once |
:::

::: callout warning | Week 5 needs a real run
Ten of week 5's fifteen points need one real run through the college gateway,
reconciled against the ledger. Any size, any outcome. It is the only row in the
course that works this way.

If the gateway is unavailable, your instructor moves the deadline. An outage
does not remove the requirement, so start week 5 on day 29.
:::

::: section | How to read a day number
Day 1 is the first day of your session. Day 7 is the Sunday that closes week 1,
and every week after that closes the same way.

Canvas turns those numbers into real dates for your section. If Canvas and this
page disagree, Canvas is right. Tell your instructor so this page gets fixed.
:::

::: section | How long a week takes
Every overview page carries an honest estimate, itemised and totalled. The
assigned work runs about 6 to 8 hours a week.

Students new to programming commonly take two to three times as long on the
parts with code in them. That puts a realistic week between about 10 and 20
hours. Plan for the larger number until you know your own pace.
:::

::: callout note | Nothing is held back for the end
There is no final exam and no final project. Week 7 is worth ten points, and it
is graded on your account of what happened rather than on whether your defences
held.
:::
