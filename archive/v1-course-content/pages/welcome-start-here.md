::: intro
This course is about agents and how they break. An **agent** is a program that
lets a language model call your code. You extend one, attack it, and prove your
fix held.
:::

::: video orientation | Day three and the list of three
Four minutes. Watch for the day 3 deadline and the three things you are ever
allowed to attack.
:::

::: steps | Your first three days
1. **Take the Module 0 Gate, due day 3.** Three parts: a quiz on the Rules of
   Engagement, a code from your own computer, and a short written answer.
2. **Read the Rules of Engagement, all of it.** The Gate quiz comes from that
   page. You need 100 percent to open Week 2.
3. **Set up the lab repository and run `python verify_env.py`.** It prints a
   short code. Paste that code into the Gate.
4. **Read the rest of this module.** The AI Use Policy and the Course Schedule
   matter most.

Then post in the Introduce Yourself discussion. It is due on day 7.
:::

::: callout stop | Why day 3 is a hard date
The Gate is your **attendance verification**, and census falls on day 8 in
almost every session.

Skip it and you can be reported as never having attended. Start on day 1.
:::

::: section | What you will be able to do
By the end of the term you can:

- Call a language model from a script and handle its errors.
- Add a tool of your own design to a working agent.
- Name three ways an agentic system gets attacked, and show one.
- Run a test harness and read what it reports.
- Write a security finding you could hand to an employer.
- Check code an AI wrote before you trust it.

The syllabus states these outcomes in the college's own words.
:::

::: section | How to move through the course
Every week has at least four pages: an Overview, a Content page, the graded
assignments, and a Wrap-up. Open the module and click **Next** at the bottom of
each page.

Lab questions go to the Q and A Discussion Board.
:::
