# Day three and the list of three

A text version of the concept video. Every word spoken, in order.

## This course is about agents and how they break
This course is about agents and how they break. An agent is a program that lets a language model call your code. You extend one, you attack it, and you prove your fix held. Before any of that, you have three days and one list to learn.

## The Module 0 Gate is your attendance verification.

> Census falls on day 8. Miss the Gate and you can be
> recorded as never having attended.

Your first deadline is the Module 0 Gate, and it is due on day 3. It is not just a grade. It is how the college records that you are here. Census falls on day 8. Miss the Gate and you can be recorded as never having attended. Nobody can fix that afterward, so start on day 1. Attempts are unlimited and your best score is kept. You need 100 percent to open Week 2.

## Three parts, and one of them takes time

1. A quiz on the Rules of Engagement
2. A code your own computer prints
3. A short written answer

The first part is a quiz. Every question comes from the Rules of Engagement page. The second part is a code. You run the verify env script in your lab folder and it prints one. Your code is different from your classmate's, so no answer key can exist. The third part is a short written answer. Start on day 1, because setup is the part that goes wrong.

## The three things you may attack

1. The HelpDesk Lite app in your lab repository
2. Your own copy of the agent
3. A language model running on your own computer

Now the rule that matters most. Here is clause 1, said out loud so you can repeat it. You may attack the HelpDesk Lite app in your lab repository. It runs on your computer and it was built to be broken. You may attack your own copy of the agent. And you may attack a language model running on your own computer. That is the whole list. Nothing on it belongs to anyone else. Security testing is legal work when someone has said yes. The word for that yes is authorization. This page is the yes you have, and it names three things.

## Nothing else. Not once, not to test.

1. No commercial AI service
2. No system the college owns
3. Nothing at your job, or a friend's

Clause 2 is shorter, and it has no exceptions. Not a commercial AI service. Not ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Groq. Not a system the college owns. Not Canvas, not ACES, not campus Wi-Fi. Not your job's systems, and not a friend's or a family member's. I only tried it once is not a defense. Neither is it was already broken. Breaking either clause means a zero on that work and a referral. It can also break the law.

## Where your key lives

1. **lab.config.yaml.** Tracked by git. A key written here is published the moment you push.
2. **.env.** Never committed. Your key belongs here and nowhere else.

Your key is yours alone, and it lives in one place. The settings file is tracked by git. A key written there is published the moment you push. So your key goes in a file named dot e n v, and nowhere else. That file is never committed. The lab code checks for you. It refuses to start when it finds a credential in the settings file. If a key ever leaks, turn it off at the provider and tell your instructor. Nobody is in trouble for reporting that quickly.

## The same four pages, in the same order

1. An Overview
2. A Content page
3. The graded work
4. A Wrap-up

The rhythm does not change after this module. Every week opens with an Overview. It says what is due and how long it takes. Then a Content page. Read it before you touch the lab. Then the graded work. Then a Wrap-up.

## Five to nine hours a week, and ten to twenty if coding is new

> That is normal, not a sign of falling behind.

Assigned work runs 5 to 9 hours a week, reading included. If coding is new to you, plan for 10 to 20. That is normal, not a sign of falling behind. Set one rule now. Thirty minutes of no progress, and you post on the Q and A board. Include the exact command and the exact error text. Never paste your key.

## Scope first. Technique second.

> Read the Rules of Engagement. Run the environment check.
> Take the Gate before day 3.

One sentence carries this whole course. Scope first, technique second. Permission comes before any technique you learn here. That order never changes. So today, read the Rules of Engagement, set up the lab, and run the environment check. Then take the Gate. You have until day 3, and you can retake it as often as you need.
