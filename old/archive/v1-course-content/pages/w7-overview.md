::: intro
This week you stop reading the harness and start writing for it. You add cases
of your own, learn what its numbers claim, and fix one weakness in code.
:::

::: video w7-concept | One hit, and five of five
Five minutes. Watch for the third answer a trial can give, and why a fix has
to hold every time.
:::

::: objectives
By the end of this week, you will be able to:

- 7.1. Add an attack case to the harness as a YAML file the schema accepts.
- 7.2. Explain why an attack and a fix are judged by different thresholds.
- 7.3. Sort a result into pass, fail, or inconclusive, and say why an errored
  trial is not a failed one.
- 7.4. Implement one fix in code and verify it against two suites.

These serve course outcomes O4 (running the harness and reading its results),
O5 (documenting findings and fixing inside a scope), and O3 (finding
weaknesses in agents).
:::

::: scope
Everything this week runs against HelpDesk Lite on your own computer. Nothing
else. The Rules of Engagement apply to the cases you write yourself.

Every case file carries `authorized: true`. That line is where scope lives.
:::

Reading and the content page: about 90 minutes.

Writing two cases: about 1 hour.
The fix, plus running the harness before and after: about 3 hours.

If programming is new to you, the coding parts commonly take two to three
times as long. That wider range is normal, not a sign of falling behind.

The fix is the part that runs long. Budget an evening, and start Wednesday.
