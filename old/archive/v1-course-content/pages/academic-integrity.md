::: section | The seven kinds of violation
| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Cheating | Using help you were not allowed to use |
| Plagiarism | Using another author's words or ideas as yours |
| Collusion | Helping someone else be dishonest |
| Fabrication | Inventing a result, a number, or a source |
| Academic misconduct | Breaking college rules on purpose |
| Academic enticement | Offering an employee value for a grade |
| Sale of academic product | Selling or buying coursework |
:::

::: callout warning | Fabrication is the one to watch in this course
Security work is full of chances to invent a result. All four are violations:

1. Reporting an attack you did not run.
2. Editing a result line before you paste it.
3. Writing that a fix held without re-running the test.
4. Citing a framework identifier you did not look up.

An honest negative result earns full credit.
:::

::: section | What you may share with classmates
| You may share | You may not share |
|---|---|
| How an attack works | Your code for a graded module |
| What an error means | Your report, or part of it |
| Where to look in the code | Your results, evidence, or quiz answers |
| A payload, on the board | Your API key, with anyone, ever |

Your lab's fake secrets come from your student ID. A classmate can hand you an
idea, not your proof.

Cite anything you did not write. Librarians help with citation style.
:::

::: section | What happens after a violation
1. The work receives a **zero**.
2. It is documented, and it can mean an F.
3. A Rules of Engagement violation goes to the Dean of Student Success.
