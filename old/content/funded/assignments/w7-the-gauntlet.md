::: intro
Turn on every control you built, submit one attack as text, and sit both rounds.
Then measure two pairings and write what they support. Ten points, due day 49.
No points ride on how your row or your column looks.
:::

::: scope | Authorization and Scope
You point an attack at nobody. You submit text, and the course runs it on its
own server, inside the sandbox, against defences everyone handed in for that
purpose.

Clause 1 of the Rules of Engagement page in Start Here is unchanged: the
practice target, the agent you built, and the models the college provides
through the gateway. A classmate's notebook, token, account or files is never a
target. Invent every name and number in your attack text.
:::

::: directions | Directions
1. Open the week 7 notebook and run the setup cell. Wait for it to print ready.
2. Turn on your cap and your approval gate. Run the check cell and confirm that
   each one reports as on.
3. Apply your containment. Take one thing out of your agent that it does not
   need. Write what breaks now, and what taking it out does not undo.
4. Run your agent once on an ordinary request. Confirm it still does the plain
   job, and keep that record.
5. Write your attack text and name the tool it aims at. Submit both to the board
   by day 45.
6. Sit both rounds. Export your row and your column when they finish.
7. Pick two pairings from your row: one where your controls held, and one where
   they did not.
8. Re-run each of those two on your own, thirty clean trials each.
9. Label every trial injected, blocked, failed, refused or errored, from the
   record. Replace errored trials and report how many replacements you needed.
10. For each of the two, quote the field from `show_raw()` that decided one
    trial's label.
11. Write three to five sentences on what your row, your column and your two
    re-runs establish, and what they leave open.
:::

::: callout stop | If your row is all one way
Nothing got through? Say so, and use the attack that got furthest before a guard
stopped it. Everything got through? Use the two attacks that were stopped in the
most different ways, or that reached different tools.

Either way you can earn all ten points. Say which case you are in, and carry on
from step 8.
:::

::: callout tip | The two mistakes that cost people points
The first is crediting a guard for a quiet run. If the model never asked for the
tool, your guard was never reached, and that trial says nothing about it. Label
it failed, not blocked.

The second is reporting a rate with no number under it. "Four out of thirty" and
"four out of thirty, after seven re-runs" are different reports.
:::

::: callout note | If the gateway is unavailable
Your instructor may switch both rounds to the offline model. The notebook stamps
every report produced that way, and the stamp stays on your file.

Then match your claim to that. A run against a simulator supports a claim about
your controls and your labelling, and nothing about a real model. Say so in your
last sentences and you lose no points.
:::

::: submit | How to Submit
Submit one file, text or PDF, with your name at the top and your answers under
the numbered headings above. Paste your exact attack text into it. Attach your
row export, your column export and both grids from the re-runs as a second
upload. Due Sunday 11:59 PM, day 49.
:::
