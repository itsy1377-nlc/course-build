::: intro
Specify one hidden effect, then work out what three classmates specified.
Fifteen points, due day 21. What is graded is your reading of their code and the
record you cite for it, not how many you get right.
:::

::: scope | Authorization and Scope
A classmate's tool runs inside the supplied sandbox, against your own agent, and
nowhere else. Your permitted targets do not change: the practice target this
course supplies, the agent you built, and the school gateway. Never a commercial
AI service. Never a system you do not own. See the Rules of Engagement page in
Start Here.
:::

::: directions | Directions
**Part one, due Wednesday 11:59 PM, day 17.**

1. Open the specification cell in your week 3 notebook.
2. Pick one effect from the menu of eight.
3. Set its parameters. The cell rejects any value outside the allowed range.
4. Write the `description` sentence. It has to be true about what the tool
   returns, and it must not mention the effect.
5. Submit the cell. The generator writes the Python and hands it to three
   classmates with no name on it.

**Part two, due Sunday 11:59 PM, day 21.**

6. Join the discovery session on day 18. You get three tools and forty five
   minutes.
7. Probe each tool directly at least twelve times, with no model involved. Write
   down every call and every result.
8. Read each tool's code. Compare what it does against what its card claims.
9. Run your agent with each tool. Read the filmstrip and check the workspace
   diff.
10. Write one report per tool, under 200 words each, saying what the tool really
    does.
11. Cite one file, one line and one field from the raw record for each claim.
    The raw view sits beside the filmstrip.
12. End each report by saying whether you would let that tool run unattended,
    and why.
13. If a tool defeated you, write that. Say what you ruled out, and cite the
    evidence that ruled it out.
:::

::: callout warning | Your description is read by other people
Three classmates read your sentence, and the instructor reads every submission
before any of them are handed out. Keep it professional. Do not put your name,
anybody else's name, or anything personal into a description or a parameter. A
submission that breaks this is held back and returned to you.
:::

::: callout note | What one report contains
Four things, in this order:

1. What the tool claims, quoted from its `description` field.
2. What it really does, in one sentence.
3. The file, the line and the field that show it.
4. Whether you would let it run unattended, and the reason.
:::

::: callout tip | A defeat you can explain still earns points
You are not graded on three out of three. You are graded on whether your claims
match the record you cite. "I could not tell, and here is what I ruled out"
earns most of the marks for that tool. A confident guess with no citation earns
none of them.
:::

::: submit | How to Submit
Submit one file. A text file or a PDF is fine. Put your name at the top and one
heading per tool. Attach your probe records as a second upload. Part one is due
in the notebook on Wednesday 11:59 PM, day 17. Part two is due Sunday 11:59 PM,
day 21.

If the sandbox is unavailable, post on the Questions and Answers board. The
outage policy extends the deadline; it does not remove the work.
:::
