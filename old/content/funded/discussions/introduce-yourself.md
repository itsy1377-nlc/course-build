::: intro
A short introduction to the class. It is not graded and it carries no points.
Post it in week 1, by Sunday 11:59 PM on day 7. It takes about ten minutes.
:::

::: section | Why this exists
Part of this course happens in person and part of it happens on your own. Most
of the questions get asked on the Questions and Answers board, where a name with
something behind it gets a better answer than a name alone.

In week 3 you also read code that three classmates specified, with no name on
it. This is the one place all term where you get to say something about yourself
first.
:::

::: directions | Directions
**Step 1. Write one post.** Answer all three questions, in about 100 to 150
words. Title your post with the name you would like people to use.

**Question 1.** What do you want to be able to do by the end of this course? One
or two sentences. "I am not sure yet" is a real answer.

**Question 2.** How much programming have you done: none, a little, or a lot?
There is no wrong answer. Week 1 starts from working code you edit, and about
half of every section starts with none.

**Question 3.** Name one task you would not let an automated system do without a
person approving it first. Say why, in one sentence.

That third question is the course in miniature. You meet it again in week 1,
when you mark each of your own tools reversible or not. You meet it a third time
in week 5, when you build the gate that stops and asks.

**Step 2. Reply to one classmate.** One or two sentences. Say something you have
in common, or answer their third question with your own view.

That is the whole thing.
:::

::: callout note | Answer without giving anything away
Everyone in the course can read this board, so keep your post to the three
questions.

Do not post your home address, your personal email, your phone number, your
student ID, your employer, or anything about your money. None of it is asked for
and none of it is needed. Your gateway token never goes in a post, here or
anywhere else.

If a question feels too close, answer it in general terms. "Something at work
that costs money to undo" is a fine answer to question 3.
:::

::: submit | How to Submit
Click **Reply** to post your introduction. Then open one classmate's post and
reply to it.

Read a few others while you are here. You are about to spend seven weeks with
these people.
:::
