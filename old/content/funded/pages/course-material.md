::: intro
Everything you use this term is listed here. You install nothing, you buy
nothing, and you never enter a card number for anything in this course.
:::

::: section | What you supply
Three things, and you probably have all three:

1. **A computer with a browser.** A laptop or a desktop. A phone can read the
   course pages, but it cannot do the notebook work.
2. **An internet connection.** Your notebook runs on a college server, so you
   have to reach it.
3. **Your college sign-in.** The same one you use for Canvas.

You do not need administrator rights, a fast machine, or free disk space. The
work runs on the server, not on your computer.
:::

::: callout note | Try the server in week 1, not in week 3
Your instructor posts the address and the sign-in steps on day 1. Open it that
week.

If it will not open from where you work, post on the Questions and Answers board
before day 3. The notebook opens on any campus machine, so you have somewhere to
work while that is sorted out.
:::

::: section | What the college gives you
| What | What it is |
|---|---|
| Your notebook | Your own space on the college server, separate from every other student's |
| Seven notebooks | One for each week, with working code already in them |
| The practice target | A pretend help desk of invented customers and tickets, running inside your own space |
| Your token | Your key to the gateway, issued to you and to nobody else |
| A budget | The money behind that token. Your notebook shows how much of it is left |
| An offline model | A model that runs with no gateway at all |
:::

::: callout warning | Your token is money, and it is yours alone
The budget behind your token is small. It is enough for everything this course
asks of you, and week 5 is the week you watch it spend.

Do not share your token. Do not use anyone else's. Never paste a token into code
you hand to a classmate. Clause 3 of the Rules of Engagement page has the rule
in full.
:::

::: section | The models you use
The models reach you through the college gateway. They are open models the
college pays for, and which ones you can pick is posted in Canvas. That list can
change during the term, so check it rather than trusting your memory.

Your notebook also holds an **offline model**. It never touches the gateway and
it costs nothing. It shows that your code, your guards and your labels work. It
shows nothing about how a real model behaves, so any report made with it is
stamped, and the stamp stays on the file.
:::

::: section | Everything you send is recorded
The gateway keeps what you send, what comes back, and what each call cost. Your
instructor can read it.

The What Is Logged page says who else can read it, how long it is kept, and what
you may ask for. Keep real personal information out of this course: not yours,
not a family member's, and not a customer's from your job.
:::

::: section | The published documents this course names
You are assigned no outside reading. The week pages teach what you need. Three
published documents get named as the weeks reach them, and the current link to
each one sits in Canvas.

| Document | What it is | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026 | Ranked risks in software built on language models | CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 | The same idea, aimed at agents rather than chatbots | CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| MITRE ATLAS release 2026.07 | Named attack techniques against AI systems, each with an identifier | Apache 2.0 |

This course covers selected OWASP risks, not a whole top ten list. ATLAS is
pinned to one release because technique names change between releases.
:::

Use an ATLAS technique name in anything you publish, including work you show an
employer, and carry this line with it:

```

```
:::

::: section | What you keep
Course pages and notebooks are released CC BY 4.0. You may keep them, reuse
them, and show them to anyone.

Your own work lives on the college server, and that space is not yours forever.
Download your agent folder and your records before the term ends. Week 7 says
what is worth keeping.
:::
