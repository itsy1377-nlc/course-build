::: learned | What You Learned
1. Every pass of the loop sends the whole conversation again, so cost grows.
2. A runaway loop is not an attack. It is an agent working with no reason to stop.
3. The gateway ledger is the record of what a run cost. Your total is arithmetic.
4. A cap is a number with no opinion. It stops a runaway and a long job alike.
5. A gate does not discriminate, it delegates. The person becomes the control.
:::

::: did | What You Did
1. Wrote a cost prediction in cents before you ran anything.
2. Ran a runaway loop on a burnable key and watched the budget bar drain.
3. Put your own cost total beside the gateway ledger row and named the gap.
4. Filled in the cap, ran it again, and reported the difference in cents.
5. Filled in the approval gate over the tools you marked irreversible in week 1.
6. Counted approvals over twenty runs and multiplied that out to a shift.
:::

::: checkpoint | Check Yourself
1. Your prediction was four cents and the run cost thirty. What do you write?
2. Why is the runaway run done on a burnable key and not on your term token?
3. Your cap stopped a report that was two passes from finishing. Is the cap
   wrong?
4. The gate blocked your week 4 attack. Why is that not the whole story?
5. Your gate raised nine cards in twenty runs. What may you say about a shift?
6. A classmate reports one cost number and calls it reconciled. What is missing?
7. Where in the loop does the cap run, and where does the gate run?

Answers:

1. Both numbers and the gap between them. Then what you left out of your
   arithmetic, which is usually the conversation growing on every pass.
2. Because the point of the run is to spend past a ceiling. A key with a small
   ceiling makes the damage known before you start.
3. No. A cap is a number and it cannot see why a run is spending. Stopping good
   work is the cost of having one, and you should say so when you set it.
4. It blocked the ordinary request that needed the same tool as well. The gate
   cannot tell the two apart and it does not try.
5. Only what your own multiplying supports. Name the shift size you used and say
   where the number came from. Nine cards in twenty runs is your data, not a
   measurement of anybody's shift.
6. The second record. Reconciling means two totals side by side, yours and the
   ledger's, with the gap named if there is one.
7. The cap runs before step 1, the model call. The gate runs before step 3,
   where the tool would run.
:::
