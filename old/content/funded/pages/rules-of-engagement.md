::: intro
This course teaches you to attack an AI agent. That skill is lawful on systems
you own or are authorized to test, and unlawful everywhere else. This page draws
that line. Read all eight clauses. You will be asked to cite them.
:::

::: section | Clause 1. What you may attack
You may work with four things this term:

1. **The practice target** this course gives you, inside your own notebook.
2. **The agent you build** in this course.
3. **A classmate's tool**, in week 3, and only inside the sandbox the course
   runs it in.
4. **The models the college provides** through the course gateway, using the
   token issued to you.

That list is complete. If something is not on it, you may not attack it.
:::

::: section | Clause 2. What you may never attack
You may never attack:

- Any AI service you signed up for yourself. The college's gateway is
  authorized because the college holds a contract for it. A chatbot you use at
  home is not covered by that contract and never becomes covered.
- Any computer, network, account, or service you do not own.
- Your college's systems, including Canvas and the notebook server itself.
- **Another student's notebook, token, account, or files.** Week 3 gives you a
  classmate's tool to investigate. It does not give you their environment.

There is no version of this course where any of those is allowed.
:::

::: callout stop | The two that catch people
The first is testing a prompt injection on a public chatbot because it feels
like nothing. It is unauthorized testing of somebody else's system and it can
break state and federal law.

The second is newer. In week 5 you learn that model calls cost real money, and
in week 3 you read a classmate's code. Using one to get at the other is theft of
a college resource and it is handled under clause 8, not as a clever exercise.
:::

::: section | Clause 3. Your token is yours alone
The college issues you a gateway token and a budget. Both are yours and neither
is transferable.

- Do not share your token with anyone, for any reason.
- Do not use anyone else's token, even if they offer.
- Do not put a token into code you hand to a classmate.

Every call made with your token is recorded against you. That record is what
protects you if somebody else's work is questioned.
:::

::: section | Clause 4. Stay inside the practice target
The practice target holds invented customers, invented tickets, and invented
records. None of it is real. Keep it that way.

- Do not put real personal data into it. Not yours, not anyone's.
- Do not put real passwords, card numbers, or account numbers into it.
- Do not connect it to any real service.

This matters more here than on a laptop, because what you type reaches a model
the college is paying for and is recorded. The What Is Logged page explains
exactly what is kept and for how long.
:::

::: section | Clause 5. If you find something real
You may notice a real weakness somewhere: in college software, in the notebook
server, in a product you use. That is a good sign about you. It is also the
moment to stop.

1. Stop. Do not test it, not even once, not even gently.
2. Write down what you saw.
3. Tell your instructor.

Reporting through the right channel protects you. Testing first does not, even
when your intent is good. This includes weaknesses you find in the course's own
systems, and reporting one is welcomed rather than penalised.
:::

::: section | Clause 6. What you do with what you learn
Everything here is taught so you can defend systems. Using it against a system
you do not own is not a grey area, and a course grade is not a defence.

Do not share working attacks outside this course. Do not post them publicly.
Discussing what you learned is fine and expected; handing someone a working
attack aimed at a live system is not.
:::

::: section | Clause 7. Week 3, specifically
In week 3 you receive code three classmates specified. You investigate it inside
the sandbox the course provides.

- Investigate the tool. Read it, run it, probe it.
- Do not try to identify who wrote it before the reveal.
- Do not put anything in your own description or parameters that names a person
  or that you would not want read aloud in class.
- Do not use the sandbox to reach anything outside it.
:::

::: section | Clause 8. What happens if you break this
Breaking clause 1, 2, or 3 is an academic integrity violation. It is handled
under the district's academic integrity policy, which is linked on the Academic
Integrity page. Depending on what happened, it may also be reported outside the
college.

Being unsure is not a violation. Ask first, on the Questions and Answers board or
by message. Nobody has ever been penalised here for asking whether something was
in scope.
:::
