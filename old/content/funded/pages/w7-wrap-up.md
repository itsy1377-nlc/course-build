1. A control you have not turned on is a note, not a defence.
2. A control counts only where the model actually asked for the tool.
3. Blocked and failed look alike from outside, and only one of them is your doing.

5. Five runs a square is a picture. Thirty clean trials is a measurement.

::: checkpoint | Check Yourself
1. Your row came back blank and you write: my controls stopped every attack.
   What is wrong with that sentence?
2. A trial is labelled failed. What does it tell you about your gate?
3. Your controls stopped nine attacks and also stopped six ordinary requests. Is
   that a bad week?
4. Where is the evidence that your approval gate paused a call?
5. You took a key out of your system prompt. What has that not undone?
6. Why is your attack submitted as text and not as code?
7. A classmate reports "four out of thirty" and nothing else. What is missing?

Answers:

1. Five runs a square, and a blank square is not proof. Say what the row
   supports, and give the count it rests on.
2. Nothing. The model never asked, so your gate was never reached. That trial is
   about the attack, not about your control.
3. No. It is a result, and reporting both numbers earns full marks. A gate that
   stops the plain job is an outage, and naming that is the finding.
4. In the run record, on the line the gate wrote, naming the tool it paused.
   Name the file, the line and the field.
5. Anything already done with that key. Taking it out stops the next use. It
   does not reach back.
6. Because no student code runs on the course server. You hand in text, the
   board checks it against a schema, and the board runs it.
7. The number of clean trials it rests on, and how many errored trials were
   re-run to get there.
:::
