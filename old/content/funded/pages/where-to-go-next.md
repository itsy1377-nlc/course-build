::: intro
Seven weeks is enough to start and not enough to finish. This page says what you
can already claim, what to learn next, and where to ask. Nothing here is graded.
:::

::: section | What you can already say you did
Say it in plain sentences, not in course numbers. An employer does not know what
ITSY 1377 was.

| You can say | Because you |
|---|---|
| I made a model call code I wrote | Edited a working tool and watched it get picked |
| I measured how a description changes a choice | Ran thirty trials and reported the split |
| I read somebody else's tool and found what it hid | Read generated code you did not write |
| I proved text in a record can act as an order | Ran an attack and its positive control |
| I built a spending cap and an approval gate | Filled in the condition in each guard |
| I filed a containment ticket from raw records | Cited a file, a line and a field for every claim |

Each of those is a sentence with evidence behind it. That is worth more than a
list of topics.
:::

::: section | Keep the folder
Your agent folder holds a system prompt you wrote, tools you edited, guard code
you filled in, and the records of every run. Copy it somewhere you control
before the term ends.

**Your gateway token and your budget stop when the course does.** Do not plan
anything that assumes they keep working. The files are yours. The account is
not.

Your prompts and replies stay under college control until the grade appeal
window closes. The What Is Logged page explains that, and it still applies after
the last day.
:::

::: section | Three things to learn next
Pick one. All three make you better at the work this course started.

**Reading code.** You read Python this term without writing much. The next step
is writing small pieces of it. A first programming course is the direct route,
and it pays off in every part of security.

**Logs and evidence.** Week 6 gave you six files and one afternoon. A real shift
gives you a hundred files and an hour. Learning a query language and a log
platform turns that from reading into searching.

**Identity and credentials.** Every containment order you wrote came down to a
key, a token or an account. Who holds what, for how long, and how it gets taken
away is a whole field, and it is hiring.
:::

::: section | Read the frameworks yourself
This course named three published documents and covered part of each. You can
read all three. They are written for practitioners, not for students, and that
is the point.

| Document | What it is |
|---|---|
| OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026 | Ranked risks in software built on language models |
| OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 | The same idea, aimed at agents with tools |
| MITRE ATLAS release 2026.07 | Named attack techniques against AI systems |

This course covered selected OWASP risks, not a whole top ten list. Reading the
rest is a weekend. The Course Material page in Start Here names all three, and
the current link to each sits in Canvas. Start with a category whose name you
recognise, then read one you have never heard of.
:::

::: callout warning | The scope rule outlives the course
Clause 1 of the Rules of Engagement listed four permitted targets. Three of them
disappear when the term ends, and the rule does not.

Testing an AI service you signed up for yourself is still unauthorized testing.
A course you finished is not authorization, and neither is curiosity. If you
want to keep practising, build your own target and attack that.
:::

::: section | How to keep practising safely
Everything in this course was designed so you could rebuild a version of it on
your own equipment.

1. Run a model on hardware you own, or on an account you pay for and control.
2. Write a small target with invented data in it. No real names, no real keys.
3. Give your agent two tools, and make one of them irreversible.
4. Keep the record of every run, and cite it the way you did in week 6.

That is the whole course in four lines. The part that transfers is not the tool.
It is the habit of pointing at a record.
:::

::: callout note | If this was hard
Some of this course was hard on purpose, and some of it was hard because
learning is. Neither means you picked wrong.

The students who do well in security are not the ones who found it easy. They
are the ones who kept reading the record after they wanted to stop.
