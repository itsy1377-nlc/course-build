::: intro
This is a course about AI, and you will use AI inside it. So the rule is not
"do not". The rule is where the line sits. An assistant may help you understand.
It may not produce your reasoning or your evidence.
:::

::: section | What you may use an assistant for
- Explain an error message you cannot read.
- Explain what a line of code does, or what a term means.
- Suggest what to check next when you are stuck.
- Fix the grammar of a sentence you already wrote.
- Make practice questions for the open book exam.

All five are permitted, and all five get declared. Using an assistant this way
is a normal working skill, and hiding it is the only part that is a problem.
:::

::: section | What you may not use one for
- Writing your answer, your analysis, or your call.
- Producing a trace, a result, a number, or a quoted record.
- Deciding what your records mean.
- Anything an assignment tells you to do yourself.

The second one is the serious one. **Evidence comes from a run you made.** Your
canary tokens come from your own student ID, so text describing a run you never
made is fabricated evidence. That is not a shortcut on writing. It is the exact
error this course exists to correct.
:::

::: section | Your records stay on your machine
Do not paste a trace, an audit log, a case pack record, or a canary token into a
commercial AI service. Everything here runs on your own computer and needs no
account, so there is never a reason to send your work anywhere.

Attacking a commercial AI service is a separate rule, and it is absolute. Read
clause 2 of the Rules of Engagement.
:::

::: section | The declaration line
Every submission ends with one line. It has three parts: which assistant, what
you asked for, and what you did with the answer.

Two examples of a complete line:

- AI use: local model on my laptop, asked what a Python error meant, then fixed
  the line myself and ran it again.
- AI use: none.

One line is enough. A grader reading it should be able to tell what was yours.
:::

::: section | The open book exam
Open book means your notes, your evidence file, and any page in this course.
It does not mean an assistant. Answer the exam yourself.

That is the one place in the course where no assistant is allowed at all.
:::

::: callout note | When the course tells you to use one
Week 5 hands you an assistant on purpose and asks for a timeline from it. That
use is the assignment, so describe it in your write-up as usual. You still open
every record it names and check the line yourself.
:::

::: section | Why the line sits here
An assistant that explains an error message teaches you something. An assistant
that writes your reasoning takes the course instead of you.

The work you are being hired for later is the checking. Practise the checking.
:::
