::: intro
Two kinds of thing break, and they go to two different places. Canvas problems
go to the help desk. Lab problems go to the board. Sending one to the other
costs you a day.
:::

::: section | Canvas problems
The district help desk owns your login. Your instructor cannot reset a password
or fix an account. The link is in the Canvas sidebar, and the Help button inside
Canvas opens the same options.

Take these to the help desk:

- You cannot sign in, or your password will not reset.
- A page or a quiz will not load in any browser.
- The course is missing from your list of courses.
:::

::: section | Set Canvas up once
1. Use a current version of Chrome, Firefox, Edge, or Safari. Keep it updated.
2. Turn on notifications for announcements and inbox messages, in your Canvas
   account settings.
3. Read the course on the mobile app. Submit files from a computer, where you
   can see what uploaded.
:::

::: section | Lab problems
The lab runs on your own computer, so the help desk cannot see it. Those go to
the Questions and Answers board instead.

Say which operating system you are on, and paste the exact error text. The
Communicating Online page lists the rest of what a good post carries.

Copy the error. Do not retype it from memory, and do not post a photograph of
your screen. One wrong character in a retyped error sends everybody looking in
the wrong place.
:::

::: callout tip | Check the walkthrough first
Most setup problems are one skipped step. Work the week 1 setup walkthrough in
order before you post. If you are past two hours, post anyway. Being stuck is
normal here, and it is not a reason to fall behind.
:::

::: section | What your computer has to do
Everything in the lab runs on your own machine: Python, a small language model,
and the practice target. The Course Material page says what you need.

After setup, the lab runs with no internet connection. Canvas still needs one,
so plan your uploads around the connection you have.

If your computer cannot run the model, message your instructor early. The course
has a supported path for that machine, and it costs you nothing.
:::

::: section | Submitting, without the last minute panic
1. Upload your file in Canvas.
2. Reopen the submission and check the file is listed.
3. Keep your own copy on your computer.

Step 2 is the one people skip, and it catches a failed upload.
:::

::: section | When Canvas is down
Your work is on your own computer, so keep working. Save your file and note
when you finished. Message your instructor once Canvas is back.

The rule on late work is on the Course Policies page.
:::
