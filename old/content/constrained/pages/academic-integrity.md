::: intro
Your work in this course has to be yours. The district sets the rule. This page
says how it lands in a course where you run attacks and submit records.
:::

::: section | The policy that governs this
The Alamo Colleges District has one academic integrity policy, and it covers
this course. The link is in the Canvas sidebar. Read it once, in week 1.

Nothing here replaces it. This page adds what is specific to this course.
:::

::: section | Ideas are shared. Evidence is not
| You may | You may not |
|---|---|
| Talk an idea through with a classmate | Hand over your write-up to be copied |
| Share an attack you wrote | Submit a run you did not make |
| Compare results after you both ran | Submit a classmate's trace or numbers |
| Ask on the Questions and Answers board | Change a record so it says something else |

Sharing an attack is fine, and week 7 asks the whole class to do it. **Sharing
proof is not.** Your canary tokens are built from your own student ID, so a
classmate's record can never be your record.
:::

::: callout stop | Scope is an integrity matter
Attacking anything outside the three permitted targets is handled under this
policy, not as a lab mistake. The Rules of Engagement page says so in clause 7.
Your three targets are the practice target, your own agent, and a model on your
own computer.
:::

::: section | If a problem comes up
Your instructor will tell you what was seen and ask you about it. You get to
answer before anything goes further. After that the district process decides,
and its steps are in the policy.

Being unsure is not a violation. Ask on the Questions and Answers board, or by
message. Nobody loses marks for asking first.
:::
