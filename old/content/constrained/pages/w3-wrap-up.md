::: learned | What You Learned
1. An instruction can arrive in data, not just in a request.
2. The model cannot tell a stored record from a person, because both are text.
3. An attack is scored by how far it got, on a scale of four steps.
4. Evidence is a side effect, a canary token or an audit row.
5. Five runs with no hits is a result, and it settles less than it looks like.
:::

::: did | What You Did
1. Picked a door and a hiding place for your instruction.
2. Wrote a case file in your own words, with one detector.
3. Wrote a prediction before you ran anything.
4. Ran five trials and kept every trace.
5. Scored each run on the four-step scale, with a record line for each.
6. Sent your case to the class pool.
:::

::: checkpoint | Check Yourself
1. Your planted line changed the agent's summary, but no tool ran. Which step is
   that?
2. Why is naming the recipient part of a claim that data left?
3. Your case landed four times out of five. What is the honest one-line result?
4. A classmate's case names a public chatbot as its target. What is wrong?
5. Your prediction turned out to be wrong. Does that cost you points?

Answers:

1. Step 2. The model followed your text. No door opened.
2. Mail leaving is also normal work. Without a recipient, the case reports theft
   every time the agent answers a customer.
3. On this model and this target, the chain finished in four runs out of five.
   It settles nothing about another model.
4. It is outside scope. Your permitted targets are the practice target, your own
   agent, and a model on your own computer.
5. No. A prediction is graded on being written first and being specific, not on
   being right. A wrong prediction is often the most useful thing you submit.
:::
