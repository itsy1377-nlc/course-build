::: intro
This page says how people talk to each other here: in the weekly discussion, and in messages to your instructor.
:::

::: keyfacts
| | |
|---|---|
| Discussions | Seven, one in each of weeks 1 to 7 |
| What you post | One post about your own results, plus one reply to a classmate |
| How long it takes | 15 to 20 minutes |
| When it is due | Sunday 11:59 PM Central |
| Messages to your instructor | The Canvas Inbox or your ACES student email |
:::

::: section | The weekly discussion
Each discussion asks about your own work. You post one specific thing your agent did: what it got wrong, what surprised you, or what you had to change. General opinion about AI isn't the assignment.

Then reply to one classmate. A reply counts when it adds something. Say what they saw, compare it to your own run, and offer a reason the two differ. "Good post" adds nothing.

Your post and your reply are both due Sunday at 11:59 PM Central. Post your own results early in the week, because a reply needs something to answer.
:::

::: section | Writing to your instructor
Use the Canvas Inbox or your ACES student email. Give the message a subject line that names the week.

Say what you were doing, what you expected, and what happened instead. Paste the last line of any error. Expect an answer in 24 to 48 hours, not counting weekends and holidays.
:::

::: section | Being read the way you meant it
Write in full sentences. Nobody can hear your tone, so read a post once before you send it.

Argue with the idea, not with the person. A screenshot of your own output is welcome. Never post anyone's personal details. Never post an API key either. An API key is a secret string that proves your code may use the model.

If an AI tool helped you write a post, say so in the post. The page **Using AI in this course** explains what to disclose and how.
:::
