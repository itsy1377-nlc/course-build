::: intro
The district names seven kinds of dishonest work. This page says what each one means in a course that runs on AI.
:::

::: keyfacts
| | |
|---|---|
| The rule of record | The district Student Code of Conduct |
| AI in this course | Allowed with disclosure. See Using AI in this course. |
| What stays yours | Your written answers, your posts, your numbers |
| If you are unsure | Email your instructor before you submit |
:::

::: section | What each one means in this course
| Name | What it means here |
|---|---|
| Cheating | Using help or materials a graded task did not allow |
| Plagiarism | Passing off another person's words, code or ideas as your own |
| Fabrication | Making up data, results or a source |
| Collusion | Working together on something meant to be done alone |
| Academic misconduct | Changing or taking graded work or exam material |
| Academic enticement or influence | Offering anything of value for a grade or an answer |
| Sale of academic product | Buying or selling coursework |

The code of conduct holds the full wording of all seven.
:::

::: reading | Where the seven come from
The Student Code of Conduct sits in the college student handbook, in the rights and responsibilities section. It starts on page 43. Read that section once. Ask yourself which of the seven names above covers anything you were unsure about.

[Northeast Lakeview College student handbook, rights and responsibilities](https://www.alamo.edu/siteassets/documents/nlc/student-services/student-handbook-2019-2020/rights-and-responsibilities.pdf)
:::

::: section | How this applies here
Using an AI assistant on your code is not cheating here. The **Using AI in this course** page authorizes it in writing. It asks one thing: add a line saying what you asked for.

Three rules follow from the list above.

1. Your numbers come from your own run. Numbers you did not produce count as fabrication.
2. Your written answer and your post are yours. Turning in what a classmate saw counts as plagiarism.
3. Labs are your own work. You can talk an approach through with a classmate.
:::

::: section | What sharing is fine
You can post an error message and ask what it means. You can compare what your agent did with what a classmate's agent did. You can point a classmate at the cell that fixed your run.

What you cannot do is send your notebook, send your export file, or write your answer from numbers that are not yours.
:::

::: callout note | Ask first
A question costs you nothing. Email your instructor with what you want to do and why.

A violation is written up, and it can cost you a zero on the work. It can also lead to an F in the course.
:::
