::: intro
This page is the tour of the course. It shows where the things you reach for most often live in Canvas.
:::

::: keyfacts
| | |
|---|---|
| Course content | **Modules**, in the left menu |
| Your scores | **Grades**, in the left menu |
| Written feedback | Inside the graded item, under your score |
| The weekly announcement | **Announcements**, in the left menu |
| Due dates | The course schedule page, and each item's own page |
| Canvas help | The **Help** button in the left menu |
:::

::: section | Modules
Click **Modules** in the left menu. Everything in this course sits there, in this order.

| Module | What is in it |
|---|---|
| Orientation Module | How the course works, the syllabus, the AI use policy |
| Student Resources | College help pages, including this one |
| Q and A Community | One open board for questions about the course |
| Module 0 | The optional Python on-ramp |
| Modules 1 to 7 | One teaching week each. Module 1 is week 1 |
| Module 8 | The final exam, and nothing else |

Every module from 1 to 7 holds the same items. An overview, a reading, a sidebar notebook, a lab notebook, a lab, a discussion, a self-check and a wrap-up. Some weeks split the reading into two pages, so those modules list nine items instead of eight.

Nothing locks except the final exam, so you can open any module at any time. The exam opens on the Monday of week 8.
:::

::: section | Announcements
Click **Announcements** in the left menu. Your instructor posts one announcement a week.

It previews the week and names that week's common sticking point. Read it before you start the lab.
:::

::: section | Grades
Click **Grades** in the left menu. It lists every graded item, what it's worth, and what you scored.

The list fills in as work is graded. Lab scores arrive within one week of the due date.
:::

::: section | Feedback
Feedback doesn't sit on the Grades list. Open the graded item itself and look under your score.

For a lab, that's the rubric with a comment on each row. A rubric is the table of criteria your work is scored against. For a discussion, the comment appears under your post. Read the rubric before you submit, not after.
:::
