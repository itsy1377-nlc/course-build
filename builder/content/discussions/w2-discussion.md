::: directions
Post by Thursday. Reply to one classmate by Sunday at 11:59 PM Central. Write about 150 words in your post. Write about 60 words in your reply.

Open your `week2_classification.json` file. Find one email where your agent needed more than one try, or where the first answer was wrong.

In your post, give all four of these.

1. The email id, plus the verdict and category your agent ended up with.
2. The exact problem your code reported on the failed try. Copy the line.
3. Your best guess at why the model answered that way.
4. One change you would make to the instructions to stop it happening again.

Then reply to one classmate. Compare their failed try to your own. Name one thing that was the same and one thing that was different. A reply that only agrees doesn't count.

You may use AI help on this post. If you do, add one line saying what you asked it.
:::

::: section | What a good post has in it
A good post is specific and short. It reads like a note you would leave a teammate.

- It names one email id, not four.
- It quotes the problem line from your own file, word for word.
- Its guess is about this reply, not about models in general.
- Its change is one sentence you could paste into the instructions tomorrow.

Two things weaken a post. One is a summary of the reading with no numbers in it. The other is a problem line copied from a classmate rather than from your own file.

A clean run is a fine post. Every email passing on try one is a real result. Say so, then give your lowest confidence number and say whether you agree with it.
:::

::: section | The reply
Read a post that isn't already answered. Compare their failed try to your own.

Name one thing that was the same and one thing that was different. Then say what you take from the difference. Sixty words is enough.

"Same here, good post" isn't a reply and earns no points.
