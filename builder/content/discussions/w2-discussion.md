::: section | What a good post has in it
A good post is specific and short. It reads like a note you'd leave a teammate.

- It names one live email id, not the saved contract test.
- It quotes a problem line from the student's own file, or clearly reports that every live row passed first try.
- It separates contract compliance from whether the verdict is right.
- Its proposed change is one sentence that could go into the instructions or reporting rule.

Two things weaken a post. One is a summary of the reading with no result from the student's file. The other is claiming that 0.85 proves the model became less sure or more accurate.
:::

::: section | The reply
Read a post that isn't already answered. Name the email and one result in that classmate's file.

Compare interpretations, not merely whether the same email retried. State one point you agree or disagree with and one tradeoff. The exact failure may be the same for both of you.

“Same here, good post” isn't a reply and earns no points.
